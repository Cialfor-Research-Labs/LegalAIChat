Transfer)

Classification
Domain: Property Law (Title Fraud) + Criminal Law (Cheating/Fraud) Issue: Double sale of immovable property (plot of land) - seller fraudulently sold the same property to two different buyers.

Key Legal Questions:
Can you claim ownership or recover the plot?
What legal action can be taken against the seller?
Is the second sale valid?
Can criminal charges be filed?

4. Fraud & Misrepresentation
If the seller knowingly sold the same plot twice, you can sue for:
Cancellation of sale deed (under the exact current statutory provision should be verified from the statute text of the Specific Relief Act, 1963).
Compensation for fraud (under the exact current statutory provision should be verified from the statute text of the Indian Contract Act, 1872).
Refund of sale amount + damages.
B. Criminal Remedies (Punishment for Seller)
The seller’s act may constitute:

1. Cheating (the exact current statutory provision should be verified from the statute text IPC / the exact current statutory provision should be verified from the statute text BNS)
Ingredients:
Deception (selling the same plot twice).
Dishonest inducement (making you pay for a property they couldn’t sell).
Wrongful loss to you.
Punishment: Up to 7 years imprisonment + fine.
Where to file: Police complaint (FIR) or private complaint in Magistrate Court.
2. Forgery (the exact current statutory provision should be verified from the statute text IPC / the exact current statutory provision should be verified from the statute text BNS)
If the seller fabricated documents (e.g., fake sale deed, fake NOC).
Punishment: Up to 7 years imprisonment.
3. Criminal Breach of Trust (the exact current statutory provision should be verified from the statute text IPC / the exact current statutory provision should be verified from the statute text BNS)
If the seller misappropriated your money (took payment but didn’t transfer title).
Punishment: Up to 3 years imprisonment.

7. Immediate Next Steps (Action Plan) #### Step 1: Secure Evidence
Collect sale deed, payment receipts, communication records.
Obtain encumbrance certificate (from sub-registrar office).
Check mutation records (from revenue department).
Take photos/videos of the plot (if in your possession).
Step 2: Legal Notice
Send a legal notice to the seller (via registered post) demanding:
Cancellation of sale deed (if fraudulent).
Refund of sale amount + damages.
Stop further transactions.
Step 3: File Police Complaint (FIR)
Go to the local police station with evidence.
File an FIR under the exact current statutory provision should be verified from the statute text IPC (BNS 318) for cheating.
If police refuse, file a private complaint in Magistrate Court.
Step 4: Civil Suit
File a civil suit in District Court for:
Declaration of title (if your deed is registered).
Cancellation of Buyer 2’s sale deed (if fraudulent).
Injunction to stop Buyer 2 from selling/constructing.
Compensation for fraud.
Step 5: Consumer Complaint (Optional)
If the seller is a developer/agent, file a consumer complaint for deficiency in service (unfair trade practice).
Step 6: Monitor Possession
If Buyer 2 is constructing, file an urgent injunction in court.
If you are in possession, do not vacate without court order.
8. Caution⚠ Do NOT take the law into your own hands (e.g., forcibly taking possession) → can lead to criminal charges (trespass, rioting).
⚠ Avoid verbal agreements - only registered deeds matter. ⚠ Act fast - delay strengthens Buyer 2’s claim.
